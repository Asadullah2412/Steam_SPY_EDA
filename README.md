# Steam_SPY_EDA
End to End EDA project

 I just solved a mystery hidden inside thousands of Steam games — and the results shocked me.

For the past few days, I’ve been deep-diving into the Steam Games dataset like a detective chasing clues:

 scraping data, cleaning chaos, plotting patterns, and connecting dots that didn’t want to connect.

And guess what?

🎮 The gaming market behaves NOTHING like we assume.

Here’s the tea I found 👇🔥

🕵️‍♂️ The Plot Twist Insights

🔥 1. Players don’t care about price — they care about FUN.

High-priced games DID NOT dominate reviews.

 It was the lower-priced, accessible titles that pulled massive player bases and racked up the most positive feedback.

🔥 2. Want great sentiment? Make games that keep players hooked.

Playtime was the strongest clue.

 The more time players spent inside a game → the more likely they were to praise it.

 Engagement > Everything.

🔥 3. Negative comments don’t target a price range — they target poor experience.

Criticism appeared everywhere: cheap games, mid-range games, even premium ones.

 The culprit? Expectations vs delivery.

🔥 4. Big publishers didn’t control the narrative.

Sure, a few giants release tons of titles…

 but smaller studios still earned massive love whenever their games delivered value.

🔥 5. The real truth emerged only when I went multivariate.

When you combine Price + Playtime + Sentiment:

 👉 Price becomes a background character.

 👉 Playtime becomes the hero.

 👉 Developers become the supporting cast.

 The story centers around experience, not “brand power.”

🎯 What This Means

Gamers don’t reward price tags.

 They reward immersion, replayability, and experiences that feel worth their time.

If a game keeps players engaged, it wins.

 If it doesn’t — even a premium label can’t save it.

Simple. Brutal. Beautiful.
